Face gestures, redesigned

A new layout for the Face gestures dialog (dialogs/face-gestures.ts), and two new controls: each of a gesture's channels can be turned off, and each one's threshold moved.

The mockups below are live. Their camera is simulated: a made-up face plays, winks every seven seconds and blinks both eyes now and then, so the meters, the ring and the page number move as they would. Drag the handles and flip the switches to try them. The gestures, numbers and trials are examples.

What is wrong now

  • It is one long column. Every gesture is fully open: its name, direction, trial count, three buttons, flags, meter, three sliders and Details (face-gestures.ts:144-209). With three gestures it runs to several screens, and the camera preview has scrolled away before you reach a gesture's sliders, though trying a gesture in place is the point of the preview.
  • On and Release are sliders under the meter they mark (face-gestures.ts:160-207). Your eye goes back and forth, and nothing shows where they ought to go. The fit knows: the rest level and each rep's peak (model.ts:72-78), but they only appear in Details, as numbers.
  • What a gesture is made of is a read-only table of six numeric columns (_face-details.ts:19-56). It's where you'd judge a gesture, and where you'd want to change it, but nothing in it can be changed.
  • A gesture's state is a stack of red lines (flags, the conflict note, the round's problem, face-gestures.ts:152-155). Nothing says at a glance whether it works.
  • The direction is a select (face-gestures.ts:140-143): two taps, and the choices are hidden until it opens.

The list

Every gesture in one row, so the list fits on a screen and the preview stays in sight.

  • Back / None / Next as three buttons, in place of the select: one tap, all three choices showing.
  • A thin live meter for each fitted gesture, with its On and Release marks, so you can watch them all at once while trying them.
  • One status line, the worst that applies (table below). The full wording is on the gesture's own view.
  • Tap a row (anywhere but the direction) to open the gesture. Add gesture… asks for a name, then opens the new gesture ready to record.

A gesture

  • The header: ‹ back to the list, the name (tap to rename, which isn't possible now) and Remove, which asks first.
  • What is wrong, at the top, in full words, with what to do about it.
  • Levels: the meter is the control. Drag On and Release on it. Release can't pass On, and dragging On below Release takes Release down too, as withLevels does now. Under the meter: the rest level and each rep's peak. A peak below On is in the warning colour, because that rep, done that way, wouldn't have turned the page. Fitted levels appears once you move one.
  • Channels replace Details. Each row has a switch, the channel's name in words, a bar with the rest's range hatched, the threshold as a handle you can drag, and a dot for its value now. The gap between the hatching and the handle shows how clear the channel is, so the Clear column goes.
  • In the example, Brow down, right has been switched off: frowning while sight-reading kept reaching it.

What changing a channel does

Turning a channel off fits the gesture again from its trials, with that channel barred. It can't be a moving channel or a cap. A cap whose moving channel is turned off goes with it, because a cap only exists to stop the gesture being done on both sides. The next clearest channel may take the freed place among the ten kept (MAX_SUPPORT, template.ts:52), and shows up as a new row.

Moving a threshold does the same, with that channel's edge (or cap) fixed where you put it; its span and clearness follow from it. While you drag, the meters and the ring score with the new threshold straight away. The fit runs again when you let go.

Why fit again rather than just take the channel out of the score: the score's scale is the levels' scale. Remove a channel or move a threshold, and the rest level and the reps' peaks move, so the old On and Release would be wrong, and so would the flags and the conflict check. Fitting again keeps them all true. So a channel change puts the levels back to the fitted ones, as recording does. Set the channels first, then the levels; the view's order says so.

Channel changes are kept across rounds: recording more trials fits again with the same channels off and the same thresholds. Clear trials keeps them too; Reset channels drops them.

Recording

As now (_face-recording.ts), with the same instructions, tones and rules, plus:

  • Five dots for the round, filled as each trial is kept.
  • A timeline of the trial under way: the six tones marked (the third of each set heavier), the held part shaded, and a moving playhead, so you can see how long each part has left without counting.
  • The instruction in large type.
  • For a gesture that's already fitted, its meter stays live. Nothing turns, as now.

What is stored

A gesture gains an optional field:

channels?: { off: string[]; at: Record<string, number> }: your changes to the template, by channel name, kept whenever the gesture is fitted again.

build(trials) becomes build(trials, channels?), and fit(frames) takes the barred channels and fixed edges. They're keyed by name, not index, so a change to the channel order can't shift them. Gestures stored without the field read as they do now, so there's nothing to migrate and the data version stays 5. FIT_VERSION doesn't change either: a gesture with no channel changes fits exactly as before.

face-gestures-spec.md would get the two views in “The settings”, the channel changes in “The gestures”, and the new field in “What is stored”. face-classifier-spec.md would get the barred channels and fixed edges in “The template”.

Decisions made here, to overrule

  1. Two views (the list, and one gesture), rather than rows that expand in one list.
  2. The meter becomes the On and Release control, and their sliders go. Hold stays a slider.
  3. Changing a channel fits the gesture again and puts the levels back to the fitted ones, rather than keeping levels you set by hand.
  4. A channel you turn off can be replaced by the next clearest one, rather than leaving the template a channel short.
  5. Channel changes survive new rounds and Clear trials; only Reset channels drops them.
  6. You can only turn channels off and move them: a “just missed” channel can't be forced in.
  7. Details' numbers (Clear, Rest reaches, each rep's peak) go, shown instead as the bars' hatching and the meter's ticks.
  8. A gesture can be renamed.
  9. Channels get names in words, with the raw name in small type under each.
  10. The gesture's view is the wide dialog (720 px); the list stays 560 px.

Questions

  1. By “adjust the thresholds”, did you mean each channel's threshold, as proposed here, or On and Release being easier to set? This does both.
  2. When you turn a channel off and another takes its place: is that welcome, or should turning one off only ever shrink a gesture?
  3. MediaPipe's Left and Right: are they your own left and right, or the picture's? The names in words have to say the right side, and that needs checking on the tablet.
  4. Should the reader say which gesture turned the page (a label beside the button, briefly)? It's outside this dialog, but it's the other place where a misfire is hard to trace.
