Face gestures, redesigned

A new layout for the Face gestures dialog (src/face/dialog.ts): a list of the gestures, and a details view for each, where its features can be turned off and their thresholds moved, and trials that went wrong left out.

The mockups are still pictures, caught while you play and wink. The gestures and numbers are examples.

The list

  • The camera preview and the face button, ring and all, as the reader shows it.
  • A fitted gesture: its name with a pencil to rename it, its direction, Record, a live meter on which On can be dragged (Release, dashed, follows), and a status line. Tap the row to open its details.
  • A gesture with fewer than three trials: only its name, the pencil, Record, and how many more trials it needs. No direction, no meter, no details. These go at the end, in the order they were added, and join the others once they have three.
  • Hold is one slider for every gesture, 100 to 1500 ms, starting at 500.

The kinds of threshold

Each feature in a gesture's template has a threshold of one of these kinds, each with its own handle. A pennant is a threshold the gesture has to reach; a bracket is a limit it must not pass, closing off the side it has to stay on.

At least

A feature that rises when you do the gesture. Below its threshold it holds the score back, fully at its rest value; at the threshold or above, not at all.

eyeBlinkRight ≥ 0.58

At most

A feature that falls when you do the gesture. The same, mirrored.

eyeWideRight ≤ 0.08

Stay under

The other side of a pair that rises, so doing the gesture with both sides doesn't set it off. Under its limit, no effect; over it, the score is cut, down to 0 where the moving side's threshold is.

eyeBlinkLeft ≤ 0.39, 0 at 0.58

Stay over

The same for a pair that falls: over its limit, no effect; under it, the score is cut, down to 0 at the moving side's threshold.

eyeWideLeft ≥ 0.14, 0 at 0.08

A gesture's details

  • The header: ‹ back, the name with its pencil, the direction, and Delete… (asks first).
  • What is wrong, in full words, with what to do, naming the trials to blame where it can.
  • On level: drag On on the meter. Release, dashed, follows. Under the meter: the rest level and each trial's held score.
  • Features: a switch, the name, a bar with its range at rest hatched, the threshold's handle, and its value now as a dot.
  • Trials: every trial, in a box that scrolls, each with a switch. A trial switched off is left out of the fit but kept, so nothing is lost and nothing needs confirming. Trial 7 is switched off here.
  • Each trial shows its score through the trial (the hold shaded, On dashed), and:
    • Held: its score while held, against a fit without it;
    • At rest: the highest its score goes at rest, but for a twentieth;
    • Fires: its frames at rest that reach On.
    A number that is wrong (held under On, at rest over Release, any that fire) is in the warning colour, and the row is shaded.
  • Show picks what the trials show: the score, or one feature (below).

Trials, by a feature

Pick a feature under Show, and each trial shows that feature through it instead of the score, with its threshold dashed, and:

  • Held: its value while held (the median);
  • At rest: the furthest it goes toward the threshold at rest, but for a tenth (what the hatching shows);
  • Past: how much of the hold it spent past its threshold.

The warning colour marks a held value short of the threshold, or a rest that reaches it. Here, trials 3 and 11 barely squinted: the squint may be worth turning off, or its threshold lowering. Every number comes from the recorded frames, which keep all 52 features, so any feature in the template can be shown.

What a change does

Turning a feature off fits the gesture again from its trials, with that feature left out. A limit whose pair is turned off goes with it. The next clearest feature may take the freed place among the ten kept, and appears as a new row.

Moving a threshold does the same, with that threshold fixed where you put it. While you drag, the meters and the ring score with the new threshold at once; the fit runs again when you let go.

Switching a trial off or on fits the gesture again from the trials in use. With fewer than three in use, it goes back to the end of the list as “Record N more trials”.

Each of these moves the rest level and the trials' held scores, so On goes back to the fitted level, and Release with it. Set the features and the trials first, then On.

Feature changes are kept across recording, switching trials and Clear trials; Reset features drops them.

Deleting is left to two things, both of which ask first: Delete…, the gesture and its trials, and Clear trials…, all of a gesture's trials, those switched off included.

Recording

Recording happens over the music, so you can play while you record. The dialog gives way to a bar at the foot of the reader's page:

  • the camera, small;
  • the instruction, as now, in large type;
  • which trial, and how many are kept so far;
  • a timeline of the trial under way: the six tones (the third of each set heavier), the held part shaded, and a playhead;
  • Stop.

Trials follow one another until Stop: those finished are kept, the one under way is thrown away, and the dialog comes back. A trial where your face wasn't seen still stops the recording, saying why.

From the reader, the music is the page you were on; pages can be turned by tap or pedal, but not by the face. From the library, with no score open, Record first asks for one to play from.

Words

What a gesture is made of. “Feature” is more natural than “channel”: “channel” is a signal-processing word. But a face's features are its eyes, brows and mouth, and what MediaPipe measures is what they do: eyeBlinkRight, jawOpen, mouthSmileLeft. The most natural word for those is movement: “turn this movement off”, “eyeBlinkRight must reach 0.58”, “the movements a wink is made of”. This page says “feature”, as you asked; it's one word to change.

Whichever wins, ARCHITECTURE.md asks for one spelling of a concept in the code, the docs and on screen alike, so it replaces channel in src/face/ and the two face specs, and channel joins the retired spellings in src/vocabulary.test.ts. Likewise a cap becomes a limit, and the gesture's button is Delete, as the vocabulary test already wants in face/dialog.ts.

What is stored, and what is dropped

  • Hold moves from each gesture to the settings: FaceSettings gains hold, 500 ms to start. Each gesture's own hold is dropped.
  • Release is no longer stored: it's worked out from the rest level and On. Releases set by hand are dropped; an On set by hand is kept.
  • A gesture gains features?: { off: string[]; at: Record<string, number> }, its feature changes by name. build(trials) becomes build(trials, features?), and fit takes the features left out and the thresholds fixed.
  • A stored trial gains off?: true. The fit is built from the trials without it, and its trial count is of those in use.
  • The numbers and traces in the trials list are worked out from the trials when the details open; nothing more is stored for them, and FIT_VERSION stays.

Decisions made here, to overrule

  1. The details also have the direction and the pencil.
  2. Trials are switched off rather than deleted, with no confirmation; Delete… and Clear trials… ask first, as now.
  3. Clear trials and Delete are only in the details.
  4. Changing a feature or switching a trial puts On back to the fitted level.
  5. A feature you turn off can be replaced by the next clearest one.
  6. Feature changes survive recording, switching trials and Clear trials; only Reset features drops them.
  7. You can't add a feature the fit left out, and Show only offers the template's features.
  8. Limits are brackets, in the warning colour; thresholds to reach are pennants.
  9. Recording is over the reader's page; from the library, it asks for a score first. Pages turn by tap or pedal while recording, never by the face.
