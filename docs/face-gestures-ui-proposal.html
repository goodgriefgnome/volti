Face gestures, redesigned

A new layout for the Face gestures dialog (src/face/dialog.ts): a list of the gestures, and a details view for each, where its features can be turned off and their thresholds moved, and bad trials deleted.

The mockups are still pictures, caught while you play and wink. The gestures and numbers are examples.

The list

  • The camera preview and the face button, ring and all, as the reader shows it.
  • Each gesture: its name with a pencil to rename it, its direction as a dropdown, and Record.
  • Once fitted, a live meter on which On can be dragged; Release, dashed, follows it.
  • A status line: the trial count, or the worst that is wrong (below).
  • Tap a fitted gesture's row to open its details. One with fewer than three trials has no details: its row only says how many more to record.
  • Hold is one slider for every gesture, 100 to 1500 ms, starting at 500.

The three kinds of threshold

A gesture's template sets a threshold on each of its features, of one of three kinds. Each kind has its own handle.

At least

A feature that rises when you do the gesture. Below its threshold it holds the score back, fully at its rest value; at the threshold or above, not at all.

eyeBlinkRight ≥ 0.58

At most

A feature that falls when you do the gesture. The same, mirrored.

eyeWideRight ≤ 0.08 (it rests around 0.20)

Limit

The other side of a pair, so doing the gesture on both sides doesn't set it off. On the near side of its limit, no effect. Past it, the score is cut, more the further it goes, down to 0 where the other side's threshold is. It points whichever way its pair moves: ≤ for one that rises, ≥ for one that falls.

eyeBlinkLeft ≤ 0.39, score 0 at 0.58

A gesture's details

  • The header: ‹ back, the name with its pencil, the direction, and Delete (asks first).
  • What is wrong, in full words, with what to do, naming the trials to blame where it can.
  • On level: drag On on the meter. Release, dashed, follows. Under the meter: the rest level and each trial's held score; one under On is in the warning colour, and named.
  • Features, one of each kind here. Each row: a switch, the feature's name, a bar with its range at rest hatched, the threshold's handle and its value now as a dot.
  • Trials, one row each (in the app, all of them), with its score through the trial (the hold shaded, On dashed), and three numbers:
    • Held: its score while held, against a fit without it (the median; what the ticks under the meter show);
    • At rest: the highest its score goes at rest, but for a twentieth (the 95th percentile);
    • Fires: its frames at rest that reach On.
    A number that is wrong (held under On, at rest over Release, any frame that fires) is in the warning colour, and the row is shaded. × deletes the trial; the gesture is fitted again.

What a change does

Turning a feature off fits the gesture again from its trials, with that feature left out. A limit whose pair is turned off goes with it. The next clearest feature may take the freed place among the ten kept, and appears as a new row.

Moving a threshold does the same, with that threshold fixed where you put it. While you drag, the meters and the ring score with the new threshold at once; the fit runs again when you let go.

Deleting a trial fits the gesture again from those left. Under three left, it goes back to the list as “Record N more trials”.

Each of these moves the rest level and the trials' held scores, so On goes back to the fitted level, and Release with it. Set the features and the trials first, then On.

Feature changes are kept across recording, deleting trials and Clear trials; Reset features drops them.

Recording

From Record, on the list or in the details. Trials follow one another until Stop: the trials finished are kept, and the one under way is thrown away. The same instructions, tones and rules as now, plus:

  • The score now, as a number, for a gesture already fitted (against its fit before this recording). Nothing turns, as now.
  • Which trial, and how many are kept so far.
  • A timeline of the trial under way: the six tones (the third of each set heavier), the held part shaded, and a playhead.

A trial where your face wasn't seen still ends the recording, saying why.

Words

“Feature” for what the code and specs call a channel. ARCHITECTURE.md asks for one spelling of a concept in the code, the docs and on screen alike, so “feature” on screen means renaming channel in src/face/ and the two face specs, and adding it to the retired spellings in src/vocabulary.test.ts. Also: a cap becomes a limit; and the gesture's button is Delete, as the vocabulary test already wants in face/dialog.ts.

What is stored, and what is dropped

  • Hold moves from each gesture to the settings: FaceSettings gains hold, 500 ms to start. Each gesture's own hold is dropped.
  • Release is no longer stored: it's worked out from the rest level and On. Releases set by hand are dropped; an On set by hand is kept.
  • A gesture gains features?: { off: string[]; at: Record<string, number> }, its feature changes by name. build(trials) becomes build(trials, features?), and fit takes the features left out and the thresholds fixed.
  • A fit gains each trial's at-rest score and its frames that fire, beside each trial's held score (peaks), so the list of trials needs no trials loaded. The trace is drawn from the trials, loaded when the details open.
  • Deleting a trial saves the trials left over its face-trials: key.
  • The fit changes what it holds, so FIT_VERSION goes up, and every gesture is fitted again from its trials when the settings are next loaded.

Decisions made here, to overrule

  1. The details also have the direction and the pencil, so both can be changed while tuning.
  2. Clear trials and Delete are only in the details.
  3. Changing a feature or deleting a trial fits the gesture again and puts On back to the fitted level.
  4. A feature you turn off can be replaced by the next clearest one.
  5. Feature changes survive recording, deleting trials and Clear trials; only Reset features drops them.
  6. You can turn features off and move them, but can't add one the fit left out.
  7. Deleting a trial doesn't ask first: it's one trial, and recording another puts it back.
  8. A trial's numbers are its held score, its at-rest score and its frames that fire, with a trace of its score; the warning colour marks held under On, at rest over Release, and any that fire.
  9. The score while recording is against the fit before the recording began; a gesture not yet fitted shows none.
  10. A limit's handle and shading are in the warning colour.
